# The Tidewatch: chapter notes

Odile Fenwright, The Salt Lanterns, volume 1. Spoilers for this whole book.

## Prologue

- Frame: ten years before the main story, Ellamere's sea wall, the night the lanterns failed.
- The Murk (a pale, creeping fog from the Grey Reach) arrives every night; 99 salt lanterns on the wall hold it back.
- The far lanterns go out one by one from the harbor mouth inward, "as if someone were walking along the wall and pinching each wick".
- Tobin Harrow goes out along the wall with a fresh lantern and a rope to relight the farthest one. His six-year-old daughter holds the rope's end at the guild steps.
- The rope stops, goes slack; hauled in, its end is cut clean. Tobin does not return.
- Faintly, out in the fog, something rings like a bell.

## Part One: The Lamp

### Chapter 1: The Ninety-Nine

- Ten years later: Nell Harrow, 16, apprentice lamplighter, wearing her father's grey coat, lights the round from lantern one (guild hall) to lantern ninety-nine (harbor mouth).
- Salt lanterns explained: brine-soaked wicks, blue-white light; ordinary fire does nothing to the Murk, salt fire makes it flinch.
- Master Corwin Ashby follows, tapping each post with his ash lighting-pole as if counting. His rule: "Ninety-nine and the Mother Lamp. Never fewer."

### Chapter 2: Brine and Wick

- Nell dips wicks in the guild cellar.
- Introduces Tamsin Reed, cartographer's apprentice, who has mapped the Murk's edge nightly for a year with her fogglass.
- Tamsin reports the edge has moved inward by the width of a street: "It's leaning on the wall. Like it's pushing at a door."

### Chapter 3: The Dark Lantern

- Lantern sixty-one is dark, not out: wick and brine fine, but the glass cracked in a spiderweb from the inside. The Murk has reached the wall there.
- Nell replaces the chimney and relights it; the fog withdraws.
- Corwin studies the glass and tells her not to tell the harbormaster, or anyone, "Not yet." (He keeps a secret; motive not given.)

## Part Two: The Fog

### Chapter 1: The Tidewatch

- Corwin takes Nell up the Tidewatch lighthouse (212 steps) to the Mother Lamp.
- Corwin: Nell's father used to sit up there with him, believed the Murk was listening, and talked to it, "Mostly he asked it to give things back."

### Chapter 2: Tamsin's Map

- In the lamp room, Tamsin shows her layered nightly pencil lines of the Murk's edge.
- Three nights the fog drew far back, all at once. She marked each in the margin: "bell".

### Interlude: The Keeper's Log

- An entry in the Tidewatch lamp-room log in Corwin's hand, dated nine years before (a year after Tobin's loss).
- He heard the bell past the harbor mouth after the second watch; the Murk drew back while it rang. "Told no one. Will tell no one."
- Reveals that Corwin has known about the bell for nine years and hidden it.

### Chapter 3: The Long Night

- A storm on the last night of autumn; the lanterns fail from the harbor mouth inward, as ten years before.
- Nell runs the wall relighting with salt matches; Tamsin calls the gaps from the Tidewatch through a speaking-trumpet.
- By midnight only the Mother Lamp holds. Corwin's hands shake too much for the brine valve, so Nell works it while Tamsin hauls brine up the steps.
- At dawn the storm passes. From beneath the water under the old drowned quay, a bell rings three times. (Ends here.)
