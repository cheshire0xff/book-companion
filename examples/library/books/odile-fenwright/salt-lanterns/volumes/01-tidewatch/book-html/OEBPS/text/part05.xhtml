Chapter 3

The Dark Lantern

Lantern sixty-one was dark when Nell reached it that evening.

Not out. Dark. The wick was wet and whole, the brine reservoir full, but the glass was cracked in a spiderweb, and the cracks had started from the inside. Beyond the wall, the Murk had come right up to the stones there, close enough that Nell could have reached into it.

She did not reach into it. She set down her satchel, unscrewed the cracked chimney, fitted a spare, and struck a salt match. The new flame took slowly, sputtering blue, and the fog drew back from the wall a step at a time, sullen, like a dog sent out of a kitchen.

Corwin came up behind her and looked at the broken glass for a long time with his good eye.

"Don't tell the harbormaster," he said. "Don't tell anyone. Not yet."
