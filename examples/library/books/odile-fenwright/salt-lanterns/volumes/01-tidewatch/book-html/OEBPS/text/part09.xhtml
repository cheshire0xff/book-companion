Chapter 3

The Long Night

The storm came in from the Grey Reach on the last night of autumn, and the lanterns began to fail.

They went out the way they had ten years before, from the harbor mouth inward. Nell ran the wall with a satchel of salt matches while the rain drove sideways, relighting what she could. Tamsin stood at the top of the Tidewatch with her fogglass and shouted the gaps down to her through a speaking-trumpet.

By midnight only the Mother Lamp held. Corwin's hands shook too badly to work the brine valve, so Nell worked it, and Tamsin fed the copper tank from buckets hauled up the two hundred and twelve steps.

At dawn the storm blew itself out. The Murk lay over the harbor, quiet and white. And from somewhere beneath the water, under the old drowned quay, a bell rang three times.
