Chapter 1


The Weather Office

The weather office of Vardo Pass was one room above the post office, with a barometer by the door and a window that looked straight down the valley.

Ivo Brandt had been its junior forecaster for three years, and its only forecaster for two. He was a long-limbed, untidy young man with a moustache he was not sure about, and he kept, pinned across the whole of one wall, a map of every small rain.

Not storms. Anyone could map storms. Ivo mapped the little rains: the shower that fell on the schoolyard and nowhere else, the drizzle that followed the miller's cart, the five minutes of rain that came every year on the baker's birthday. Each one was a pin with a paper flag, dated in his careful hand.

The mayor thought the map was a waste of pins. Ivo thought it was the only honest record of the weather in the valley.





Chapter 2


Rain on One Street

In the second week of June, it rained on Cooper Street and nowhere else.

It began on a Monday, a soft steady rain that stopped exactly at the corner by the chandler's shop. On one side of the line, the cobbles were wet and the gutters ran. On the other side, a boy sat in the sun eating an apple.

By Thursday the residents of Cooper Street were sleeping in their neighbours' houses, and the rest of the town had come to stare. Ivo stood at the corner with his notebook and his rain gauge and a borrowed umbrella, and measured, and measured, and could not make the numbers mean anything.

The rain fell hardest outside number nine, a narrow blue house with its shutters closed, where, the neighbours told him, a woman had moved in the week before and had not been seen since.





Chapter 3


The Woman with the Umbrella

Ilka Varga answered the door of number nine on the third knock. She was small and elderly, in a raincoat buttoned to the chin, and she held a black umbrella open over her head, indoors.

"I'm sorry," she said, before Ivo could explain himself. "It follows me. It always has. I thought the mountains might be too high for it."

She had carried her own weather all her life. It had cost her three houses, a husband, and a career as a concert pianist, because no hall would have her twice. She had come to Vardo Pass because someone had told her that the weather here was strange already, and that one more strangeness might not be noticed.

Ivo looked at the rain sheeting down past her window, and thought of his wall of pins.

"Would you mind," he said, "if I made a map of it?"





Chapter 4


Clearing

It took Ivo the rest of the summer, and every pin in the post office.

He mapped Ilka's rain by the hour: how it thinned when she laughed, thickened when she remembered her piano, and stopped altogether, for eleven minutes one August evening, while she sat on the weather-office steps and listened to the town band practising below.

By autumn he had a theory, and by winter Ilka was playing the old upright piano in the schoolroom every afternoon while the children did their sums. It still rained on her, a little, most days. But it was the small, soft kind of rain that the valley had always had, and Ivo gave it a pin and a flag of its own, like any other.
