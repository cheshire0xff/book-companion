Chapter 4

Clearing

It took Ivo the rest of the summer, and every pin in the post office.

He mapped Ilka's rain by the hour: how it thinned when she laughed, thickened when she remembered her piano, and stopped altogether, for eleven minutes one August evening, while she sat on the weather-office steps and listened to the town band practising below.

By autumn he had a theory, and by winter Ilka was playing the old upright piano in the schoolroom every afternoon while the children did their sums. It still rained on her, a little, most days. But it was the small, soft kind of rain that the valley had always had, and Ivo gave it a pin and a flag of its own, like any other.
